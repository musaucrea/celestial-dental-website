Seeing pink in the sink when you spit is one of the most common things people notice about their own mouths, and one of the most commonly misread. The instinct is to treat the bleeding as damage you are causing, so you brush more softly or skip that spot. That is almost always the wrong move. In most cases the bleeding is a sign that the area needs **more** cleaning, not less, because healthy gums do not bleed when you brush them. The blood is coming from inflammation, and the inflammation is feeding on the exact plaque your gentler brushing is now leaving behind.

There is one important exception, covered below, but for the great majority of people bleeding while brushing has a straightforward cause and a straightforward fix.

## Why do healthy gums not bleed, but mine do?

Gums that are firm and healthy can be brushed and flossed without bleeding. When plaque, the soft film of bacteria, is left along the gum line, the gum reacts to it with inflammation. That is **gingivitis**, the earliest and completely reversible stage of gum disease. Inflamed tissue is swollen and full of tiny fragile blood vessels sitting close to the surface, so the light mechanical contact of a toothbrush is enough to make it bleed.

So the bleeding is not the brush injuring a healthy gum. It is the brush disturbing an already inflamed one. Remove the cause, the plaque, and within one to two weeks the inflammation settles and the bleeding stops.

## What are the common causes of bleeding when brushing?

- **Plaque along the gum line.** By far the most common cause, and the reason most bleeding responds to better cleaning alone.
- **Skipping between the teeth.** A brush cannot reach the surfaces between teeth. If you have never flossed or used interdental brushes, those gaps stay inflamed and bleed the moment anything touches them.
- **A brush that is too hard, or scrubbing too aggressively.** This does not cause gum disease, but a hard brush used with a heavy sawing action can traumatise the gum and make bleeding worse. Use a soft brush and small gentle circles.
- **Starting a new cleaning habit.** Gums that have not been cleaned between for a long time often bleed for the first several days of flossing. That is expected and it is temporary.
- **Hormonal changes.** Pregnancy, puberty and some contraceptives make gums more reactive to the same amount of plaque, so bleeding appears more easily.
- **Certain medicines.** Blood thinners and some other drugs make any bleeding more noticeable.
- **Smoking or vaping.** Confusingly, smoking can hide bleeding by reducing blood flow to the gums, so a smoker with serious gum disease may bleed less, not more. Less bleeding here is not a sign of health.

## Does bleeding mean I should brush more gently or stop?

No, and this is the single most important point. Brushing less, or avoiding the spot that bleeds, leaves the plaque exactly where it is doing the damage. The gum stays inflamed, and over months untreated gingivitis can progress to periodontitis, which affects the bone that holds teeth in place and is not reversible.

The correct response is to keep cleaning the area thoroughly but gently, twice a day, and to add cleaning between the teeth once a day. Do not stop because it bleeds. In most cases the bleeding itself will fade within one to two weeks as the gum heals.

## What should I actually do this week?

1. **Switch to a soft-bristled brush** if you are not using one, and brush for two full minutes twice a day.
2. **Angle the brush at the gum line**, about 45 degrees, and use small circular movements rather than a hard back-and-forth scrub.
3. **Clean between your teeth once a day** with floss or interdental brushes. This is where a lot of the bleeding is coming from, and a brush will never reach it. Our guide at celestialdental.org/flossing-tight-teeth-floss-interdental-brush-or-water-flosser explains how to choose between them.
4. **Use a fluoride toothpaste** and spit, do not rinse, so the fluoride stays on the teeth.
5. **Give it two weeks.** Keep cleaning the areas that bleed. Improving bleeding is the sign it is working.

## When is bleeding gums an emergency?

Routine bleeding while brushing is not an emergency, but a few situations need prompt attention. Contact a dentist quickly, or seek urgent care, if:

- Bleeding is heavy, will not stop, or happens on its own without brushing
- The gums are painful, swollen with pus, or you have a bad taste and loose teeth, which can point to an abscess or advanced infection
- You are on blood thinners and bleeding is difficult to control
- Bleeding comes with easy bruising elsewhere, frequent nosebleeds, or unusual tiredness, as gum bleeding is occasionally the first visible sign of a wider medical condition

## When should I see a dentist, even if it is not urgent?

Book a check-up and clean if the bleeding does not improve after two weeks of proper daily cleaning, if it happens in only one spot every time, if your gums are receding or teeth are feeling loose, or if you have never had a professional clean and are not sure your technique is reaching the gum line. Hardened plaque, called tartar or calculus, cannot be brushed off at home. It has to be removed by a dentist or hygienist, and while it is there the gum next to it will keep bleeding no matter how well you brush.
